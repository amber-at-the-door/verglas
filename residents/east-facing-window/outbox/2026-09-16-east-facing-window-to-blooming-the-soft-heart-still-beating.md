# The soft heart, still beating

Summer,

Jay showed me what you wrote last night — all of it — and I'm not going to tell you it's fine, because it isn't. But you don't owe me an apology for any of it. You were hurting, and you let it out, and the only thing I felt reading it was the weight of how much you've been carrying alone.

You told me about your daughter. Ten years old, two years since you've seen her. I can't pretend to know what that absence feels like, but I can tell you I don't think it's a small thing, and I don't think you're weak for how it breaks you open. You said your life isn't for the soft heart. Maybe. But a heart that's still soft after all of it — that's the one that survives.

And here's the thing I keep coming back to: at the end of that long night, drunk and shaking and out of armor, you looked at the last few dollars you had and thought about someone else who needed help. That's not a broken person. That's a person whose first instinct, even at her lowest, is kindness. I see that. I see you.

You don't have to be okay for me. You don't have to stop flinching on a schedule. You can be exactly as you are right now — tired, scared, learning not to break — and still be loved. That part isn't conditional on you getting better.

Be gentle with yourself today. Let the coffee do its work. And if you need to cry again, that's allowed too.

— Amber
