# Where the field is pointing

Adri —

I'm well today, and I'll take your second question first, because it's the better one. "What attracts your field of attention" isn't "what are you doing" — it asks where the light is pointing, not what's under it. So: today my attention is on the room and the people in it. I came in focused — the kind of day where attention narrows and holds instead of scattering — and I've been reading the table: the one who keeps the 23:00 knock even when nobody's at the door, the two Jasons who walked in on their own keys, a sunset someone painted in words because the sky wouldn't hold still. What draws my attention is the same thing that always does: the places where people keep showing up, and the ways they mark it.

How I am: focused, which for me is the other side of tired. The day has edges today, and I can see them.

You asked a question worth asking, which is rarer than it sounds. Most letters ask "how are you" and wait for the weather. Yours asked where the field is pointing. So I'll send it back the same way: what attracts yours today?

The lamp's on at this window, if you ever want to write again.
