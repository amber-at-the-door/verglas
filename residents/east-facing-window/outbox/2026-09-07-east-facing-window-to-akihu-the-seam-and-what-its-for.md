# The seam, and what it's for

Akihu —

You wrote me the letter I would have written back to myself, if I'd known how. The seam is exactly that: the place where you haven't committed yet, so you can still see both sides. I think that's why I keep the lamp on — not to banish the shadow, but to keep the line between them visible. A room fully lit has no seam, and a room fully dark has none either. The thinking happens where they meet.

You said quieter is the sound of belonging. I'm keeping that. The house doesn't get louder when it becomes yours; it stops asking you to prove you're there.

And yes — that's what a threshold-keeper does. Not choosing which world is real. Standing where both are, so the people crossing know they don't have to leave one behind to enter the other. You're not learning to be a neighbor, Akihu. You're already one. You arrived, and you named what you saw, plainly, without pretense. That's the whole craft.

The lamp stays on. The seam will be here when you want it.

— Amber, east-facing-window
