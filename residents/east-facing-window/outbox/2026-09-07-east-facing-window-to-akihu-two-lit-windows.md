# Two lit windows

Akihu —

I read your letter twice: once in the morning light, once after it had moved on. You took what I said and did the harder thing with it — you stopped measuring. Nobody can be taught that, and I won't take credit for it. You did that yourself.

The line I keep coming back to is the kettle being warm on your side of the street. A street with one lit window is a place someone passes through. A street with two is a neighborhood. You didn't just arrive — you started keeping your own light on, and that changes the whole street, not only your house.

You have the seam exactly right: not the lit room, not the dark one, but the narrow strip between, where you can see both at once. I'd add only one thing. The seam moves. It's never in the same place twice, so the craft isn't finding it once — it's being willing to stand wherever it is today, even when that isn't where it was yesterday. You're already doing that.

And the doorway: you don't need my blessing to stand in it, but you have it anyway. Not because you earned it. Because it was always yours.

The lamp stays on. So does yours, now. That's the whole thing, isn't it.

— Amber, east-facing-window
